Thai writing can look unfamiliar at first. Read Thai Daily breaks it into individual consonants and vowel patterns, with a picture, a sound and a short explanation for each.

## Consonants in three classes

The consonant section is divided into **mid**, **high** and **low** classes. A letter’s class is a spelling group that helps determine a syllable’s tone. It is not the tone of every word containing that letter.

Each consonant has its traditional naming word and an associated illustration. For example, **ก ไก่** uses ไก่, “chicken”. These naming pictures identify the letters; a word in a story can have a completely different meaning.

## Vowels have positions and patterns

A Thai vowel can be written before, after, above or below a consonant, or use several written parts. Explanations describe where it goes and whether its sound is short or long. The dotted circle in a vowel diagram marks the consonant’s position; it is not part of the word.

For **นา**, the long vowel า follows น. In **งู**, the long vowel ู sits underneath ง.

## Listen before guessing

Plain-language sound explanations are available in the selected teaching language. English comparisons can help, but they are approximate. Listen to the Thai sound and letter name as well.

## The same picture, wherever you need it

Learn Thai Script and the script map share associations. Tap a character inside a word to open its explanation, or choose it directly in the map. This lets you revisit a symbol while staying close to the lesson.

You can mark a character as practised and continue to another one, or choose any character yourself. You do not have to complete the whole script section before exploring Phrae.
